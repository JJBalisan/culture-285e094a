Loxwood tickets and Jazz Festival nights

Welcome to Culture, the week in London. This week's best picks. The Kingdom of Loxwood has put twenty twenty-seven tickets on sale. Zamar-Eden plays a jazz night at Morocco Bound on Saturday the fourteenth of November, as part of the E F G London Jazz Festival. Twerk Temple, a night for Black and Brown queer, trans and femme people, is at Dalston Superstore on Friday the thirteenth. And Nabil Abdulrashid has added an extra show at the Top Secret Comedy Club on Monday the sixteenth.

It is a thin week for film listings, and I have left out anything I could not confirm. The window runs from Friday the sixth of November to Sunday the fifteenth, with a few dates further ahead.

Queer events

First, the week's queer nights.

The Boy Is Mine, from the Handsome House Party, is at the Eagle in Kennington on Friday the sixth of November, from nine p m to four a m. It is R and B for gay men, with a B I P O C focus, for eighteens and over, and the venue is step-free. Tickets run from five to eight pounds plus a booking fee, and last entry is half past two.

Twerk Temple, from Popola, is at Dalston Superstore on Friday the thirteenth, from ten p m, for eight pounds fifty. The music is reggaeton, dancehall, soca and Afro rave, and the organisers call it a space for Black and Brown diasporic queers, trans folks and femmes. Eighteens and over.

On Saturday the fourteenth, Handsome throws its fourteenth birthday bash, again at the Eagle, for eight pounds.

Further ahead, the Riot London end of the year party is at Electrowerkz on Sunday the twenty-seventh of December.

One cancellation to note: the QueerSwap clothing swap at Deptford Lounge, planned for Saturday the seventh, shows as cancelled on the organiser's ticket page.

One-offs

Now the week's one-offs and unusual events, taken from the Londonist weekend listing. I have not checked prices unless I say so.

Arcadia, by the artist and film-maker John Akomfrah, opens at the Natural History Museum on Friday the sixth of November, and it is free. It is a film installation about the Columbian Exchange.

At the Design Museum, N U E: Black Aesthetic opens on the sixth and runs until the eighth of August next year. It shows contemporary Black designers.

Lost Worlds, by Luxmuralis, fills the Old Royal Naval College in Greenwich with light and sound after dark, from the sixth to the fifteenth of November.

Charlie Mackesy has an exhibition at Dulwich Picture Gallery, running until the seventh of February.

Through Our Eyes, on Sámi art and culture, opens at Somerset House on the sixth.

Blackheath Fireworks are on Blackheath Common on Saturday the seventh, running until ten p m. The ticket price was not stated.

And the Voila Theatre Festival continues across London until the twenty-second of November, with close to one hundred productions.

Date ideas

A few ways to put those together.

Start with jazz. Zamar-Eden plays Morocco Bound on Saturday the fourteenth, doors at seven, for fifteen pounds. It is part of the London Jazz Festival, which runs from the thirteenth to the twenty-second of November. Other verified shows include the Dele Sosimi Afrobeat Orchestra at Soul Mama in Islington on the fourteenth at half past eight, for twenty-eight pounds sixty-eight, and After John, a tribute to the cosmic music of Alice Coltrane, at the Vortex on Friday the thirteenth at half past eight, for twenty-two pounds. Later in the festival, Ego Ella May plays Ronnie Scott's on Wednesday the eighteenth, for forty to fifty-five pounds, and Kaidi Akinnibi with Mackwood plays Kings Place on Saturday the twenty-first, for twenty-five pounds fifty.

For something free and visual, pair the Akomfrah film at the Natural History Museum with the N U E exhibition at the Design Museum. Or see the Lost Worlds light show at the Old Royal Naval College in Greenwich after dark, then eat in Greenwich.

For food, the Roobarbs plant-based supper at Four Boroughs in Crystal Palace is on Friday the sixth.

On sale soon

The big news is that the Kingdom of Loxwood Joust has put twenty twenty-seven on sale. It runs over three weekends in August: the seventh and eighth, the fourteenth and fifteenth, and the twenty-first and twenty-second, at Loxwood Meadow.

Day passes come in three tiers. Early Bird is thirty-two pounds for adults and eight pounds for children, with five hundred a day. Advanced is thirty-seven pounds and nine pounds, with fifteen hundred a day. Final Release is forty-two pounds and ten pounds. Weekend camping is one hundred and thirty pounds, with seven hundred places a weekend, and about a third are already gone. The headliner is Fortress Dwellers, in their first European show. The tickets page also lists a grand banquet as an add-on. Its price and seating numbers were not shown, so check the booking flow before it sells out.

Robert White has added an extra show at Top Secret on Sunday the twenty-second of November at three p m, with limited tickets at twenty pounds. The evening show has sold out.

And booking for the DINE supper club opens on Thursday the nineteenth of November at six p m on Eventbrite. More on that in the food section.

Smaller theatres

Six plays opening at London theatres. I have not checked ticket prices for any of them.

ATTENTION, co-created by Jules Head and Georgia Bruce, runs in the Bush Theatre Studio from the nineteenth of November to the twenty-third of December. It has been described as a queer play.

Global Majority, by Chris Fung, plays in the Bush's main space from the twenty-fifth of November to the sixteenth of January.

The Afronauts, by Ryan Calais Cameron, with Lucian Msamati, runs at the Royal Court from the fourteenth of November to the nineteenth of December.

Desire Under the Elms opens at the Almeida on the tenth of November and runs until the twenty-third of December. Ebenezer Bamgboye directs, with a Black family cast as the Cabots.

Nero, Duncan Sheik's final musical, is at Southwark Playhouse Elephant until the fifth of December.

And Cloud Nine, the Churchill revival about sexual and colonial repression, is at the National Theatre's Lyttelton until the thirteenth of January.

Films

A quiet week here, because release dates were not confirmed, so I will stick to what is.

The Prince Charles Cinema is showing Ken Russell's The Devils, the director's cut, on thirty-five millimetre. The Thursday showing sold out, and other dates exist. Evening tickets are eleven pounds fifty or fourteen pounds fifty, plus a seventy-five pence fee.

At BFI Southbank, look out for the twentieth anniversary of Pan's Labyrinth and a Billy Wilder season. I do not have exact dates for either. The London Film Festival has now finished.

Food

Plant-based food this week.

The Crystal Palace Food Market on Haynes Lane runs on the first and third Saturdays, so the seventh and the twenty-first of November, with a plant-based stall from Roobarbs.

Roobarbs Supp'r is at Four Boroughs in Crystal Palace on Friday the sixth of November: three courses, fully plant-based, for forty pounds. Festive suppers follow on several dates in December, also at forty pounds. Four Boroughs is a brewery, and I could not confirm whether the ticket is food only. Roobarbs also runs a festive supper club at Gipsy Hill Taproom, on a shared long table, on Tuesday the eighth and fifteenth of December, for thirty pounds. Availability was not checked for either.

DINE at The Bridge is plant-based, pay what you can, with mixed tables, on the first Thursday of each month from six to half past eight in the evening. The next is Thursday the third of December, and booking opens on Thursday the nineteenth of November at six p m on Eventbrite.

One new opening. Botanical Boys The Bistro opens at Coal Drops Yard, Unit seventy-six, on Wednesday the eleventh of November. It is a collaboration between The Holy Kitchen and Botanical Boys, open Wednesday to Saturday, from eleven in the morning to nine at night. The menu has baguettes, hot pots such as plant-based lasagne and butter chickpea curry, a cake counter and evening small plates. It is described as plant-based. That is from a single source, dated the third of November.

Updates on two places still to come: Tofu Vegan on the Strand has no confirmed date, and Karam's in the Brunswick Centre, which is vegetarian, is still delayed.

Comedy

Finally, comedy.

Nabil Abdulrashid brings Urban Battuta to the Top Secret Comedy Club on Drury Lane on Monday the sixteenth of November at seven p m. It is an extra show, with limited tickets at fifteen pounds. He is a Muslim Black British comic known for Have I Got News For You and Live at the Apollo.

Robert White, a gay, autistic keyboard comic, plays Top Secret on Sunday the twenty-second at three p m, with limited tickets at twenty pounds.

Top Secret ran a one pound entry night on Monday the second of November, with Andrew Mensah on the bill. It is worth checking whether it repeats.

Paul Foot brings The Future to Soho Theatre on the twenty-third and twenty-fourth of November, and the second to the fifth of December, at seven fifteen p m. A search listing gave prices of twenty-three to twenty-seven pounds, but the theatre's own page was not confirmed.

And a Show and Tell mailing lists Dan Tiernan on Sunday the eighth of November, Tatty Macleod on the twentieth, Austentatious on the twenty-third, and Nina Conti later in the month. Venues and prices were not read.
