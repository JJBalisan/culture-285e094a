The Afronauts and Loxwood tickets

Welcome to Culture, the week in London. This week's best picks. Loxwood Joust twenty twenty-seven tickets are on sale, with day passes from thirty-two pounds. The Royal Court opens The Afronauts, a world premiere about the Zambian space programme, on Saturday the fourteenth of November. And Nabil Abdulrashid has added a Monday night show at Top Secret on the sixteenth. Also worth a diary entry: booking for the DINE supper club opens on Thursday the nineteenth of November at six p m.

One heads-up first. Londonist reports eight twenty-four hour Tube strikes planned between November and February. I could not find the dates, so check before you travel. The window this week runs from Friday the thirteenth to Sunday the twenty-second of November, with some things further ahead.

Queer events

Starting with the week's queer nights.

Twerk Temple, from Popola, is at Dalston Superstore on Friday the thirteenth, ten p m until four a m, for eight pounds fifty plus a small fee. It is a queer, POC centred night of reggaeton, dancehall, soca, baile funk and Afro rave.

Comedy in Ruins is at the Forty Foot Brewery in Blackhorse Road on Friday the thirteenth. Doors are at seven p m, and the show runs from half past seven to ten. It is a P O C and queer led night, F L I N T A plus, eighteens and over, and step-free. Tickets are income based, from eleven pounds fifty to fourteen pounds fifty, and down to four pounds fifty. It is hosted by Emily Bampton.

Also on the thirteenth, the Riposte queer rave takes over Electrowerkz from nine p m until six a m, with two dance floors plus a performance and art room. The price was not listed when I looked.

On Saturday the fourteenth, the Roadrunner gay run club meets at Potters Fields Park from ten a m, and it is free.

On Sunday the twenty-second, the Wormhole Market in Hackney Wick is a free queer makers' market, from midday to seven p m.

Further ahead, Dorothy's, a lesbian and trans centred pub in Camberwell, plans to open towards the end of November, with supper clubs and hobby groups in the plans. No date has been given. Club ARE celebrates its third anniversary at Hackney Bridge on Saturday the twenty-eighth of November, with tickets from fifteen pounds fifty to twenty-seven pounds fifty. And Comedy in Ruins has a Christmas show at the Forty Foot Brewery on Friday the eleventh of December, from four pounds fifty.

One-offs

The E F G London Jazz Festival runs from the thirteenth to the twenty-second of November, at venues across London, and many events are free. Names to look out for include the Dele Sosimi Afrobeat Orchestra, Zamar-Eden, Ego Ella May, and a set of Alice Coltrane music from After John. At the Barbican, there is a Take Five Showcase on Saturday the twenty-first at five p m, and Church of Sound turns ten with a night on Friday the twentieth at half past seven.

Also at the Barbican, on Sunday the twenty-second, anaiis and Tayo Rapoport present devotion, with film, music and movement. It is the Conservatory's final public performance before its renewal.

At the Horniman Museum in Forest Hill, Meeting Mohini is a free display about the Koovagam festival and Indian transgender culture, and it closes on Sunday the fifteenth. On Wednesday the eighteenth, from six to half past eight in the evening, Walrus Returns marks the return of the museum's famous walrus. The price was not stated.

The London Palestine Film Festival runs from the thirteenth to the nineteenth of November at the Barbican and other venues.

And the new London Museum opens at Smithfield on Saturday the twenty-eighth of November, with late openings on Fridays and Saturdays. Flexible entry tickets start from fifteen pounds.

Date ideas

A few ways to put a night together. The Horniman is a good evening out: Walrus Returns on the eighteenth, then the aquarium and gardens, with free entry to the museum itself.

There is a free queer life drawing session at the West End Arts Library on Friday the thirteenth, from six to eight p m.

The Firefly Festive Forest is a free outdoor trail of more than thirty Christmas tree sculptures along Sloane Street, from the twelfth of November to the first of December. It is ten minutes from the Royal Court, so it pairs well with The Afronauts.

Or try the Roadrunner run on Saturday the fourteenth, then a walk along the river.

On sale soon

Loxwood Joust twenty twenty-seven is on sale. The festival days are the seventh and eighth, the fourteenth and fifteenth, and the twenty-first and twenty-second of August, at Loxwood Meadow in West Sussex. A weekend camping pass is one hundred and thirty pounds, with seven hundred places a weekend, and about a third are already sold. New tiered day passes start with the early bird at thirty-two pounds for an adult and eight pounds for a child, then thirty-seven and nine pounds, then a final release at forty-two and ten. The headliner is Fortress Dwellers, in their first European show, and there is a new Woodland Fantasy Rave on Saturday nights for camping pass holders. The Feast of Champions banquet is listed as an extra and is said to sell out every year, but I could not confirm that sittings are on sale yet.

The DINE supper club on Thursday the third of December opens booking on Eventbrite at six p m on Thursday the nineteenth of November.

Soho Theatre has announced Josh Sharp with An Hour of Crowd Work, at Dean Street from the fifteenth to the twenty-seventh of February, plus a show at Soho Theatre Walthamstow on Saturday the sixth of March. Prices were not confirmed.

The Barbican has put Mon Laferte on sale for Wednesday the twenty-seventh of January. And Visit London says to expect Black Friday theatre deals in the last week of November.

Smaller theatres

The Afronauts opens at the Royal Court Downstairs on Saturday the fourteenth of November and runs to Saturday the nineteenth of December, with press night on Monday the twenty-third. It is Ryan Calais Cameron's world premiere about the nineteen sixties Zambian space programme, led by Lucian Msamati, with a Black British and Black African cast. Tickets run from fifteen to seventy-four pounds fifty, and it is listed as selling out.

Nine Night at the Kiln Theatre in Kilburn runs until the second of January, from nineteen pounds. Natasha Gordon's play about a Jamaican wake is directed by Amit Sharma.

Global Majority opens at the Bush Theatre on Wednesday the twenty-fifth of November, from twelve pounds. It is a new play by Chris Fung, billed as Black Mirror meets Succession.

Fly, You Fools! opens at Southwark Playhouse Borough on the twenty-fifth of November. A preview on opening night is ten pounds, other previews sixteen pounds, and standard tickets thirty-five.

Eurotrash opens at the Young Vic on Friday the thirteenth, and runs to the ninth of January.

Films

On Friday the thirteenth of November, the one to look for is Fjord, with Renate Reinsve and Sebastian Stan. There is also Ebenezer, with Johnny Depp as Scrooge, and a fiftieth anniversary release of The Man Who Fell to Earth.

On Friday the twentieth, The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping arrives. There is also You Can See Everything from A twenty-four, with Nathan Fielder and Elizabeth Holmes, and Josephine, with Channing Tatum and Gemma Chan.

On the independent side, La Bola Negra, a queer epic inspired by Lorca, runs at the Barbican until the nineteenth. And the Barbican's season of films by William Greaves, I Am Furious Black, runs to the twenty-eighth.

Food

On the vegan food front, it is a quiet week for new places.

Tofu Vegan has opened on the Strand, in the former Real Greek site. It is fully vegan, with a gluten-free Chinese menu. It is reported as open since the first of November.

The Chelsea Physic Garden Christmas Fair runs from Thursday the twenty-sixth to Sunday the twenty-ninth of November, with tickets at eight pounds booked in advance.

For supper clubs, DINE at The Bridge Social near Southwark Bridge is on Thursday the third of December, from six to half past eight, with last entry at half past six. It is plant-based, pay what you can, with shared tables. Booking opens on the nineteenth.

Comedy

Nabil Abdulrashid brings Urban Battuta to Top Secret on Drury Lane on Monday the sixteenth of November at seven p m, with limited tickets at fifteen pounds. He is a Black British comic from Live at the Apollo and Have I Got News For You, and this is an extra date added due to demand.

Robert White, the gay autistic keyboard comic, has an extra afternoon show at Top Secret on Sunday the twenty-second at three p m, with limited tickets at twenty pounds, after the evening sold out.

At Soho Theatre, Ayo Adenekan brings Homeland Insecurity from Wednesday the twenty-fifth to Saturday the twenty-eighth. Temi Wilkey's Lover Girl runs to Saturday the twenty-first, from nineteen pounds. Frankie Thompson's clown show Horrible Things runs to the same day, from thirteen pounds. And Sofie Hagen is on to Saturday the fourteenth, from twenty pounds.

Phil Wang plays Soho Theatre Walthamstow on Wednesday the eighteenth, and Neon Nights with Michelle De Swarte is there on Saturday the twenty-first.

At the Comedy Store, Kae Kurd is on the bills on Friday the twentieth and Saturday the twenty-first, with Jordan Gray on the twenty-sixth and Chris McCausland on the twenty-seventh.

For something cheap, J K Comedy Club in Covent Garden and Soho lists shows from eight to fifteen pounds most nights.
