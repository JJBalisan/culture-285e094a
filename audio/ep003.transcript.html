Loxwood tickets and Nabil Abdulrashid

This is Culture, a spoken guide to the week in London, from Friday the thirtieth of October. It is a thinner week than usual, and I will say so where it matters.

Three picks lead the way. First, tickets for the twenty twenty-seven Loxwood Joust are live now, with an early bird day pass at thirty-two pounds. Second, the comedian Nabil Abdulrashid has added a night at the Top Secret Comedy Club on Monday the sixteenth of November, with a limited run of fifteen pound tickets. And third, two plant-based suppers: a pay what you can dinner at The Bridge Social on Thursday the fifth of November, and a Supper Club at Gipsy Hill Taproom on Tuesday the third.

Queer events

Not a big week for queer listings, but there are a few dates worth having.

Riot London Halloween is at Electrowerkz on Sunday the first of November, with tickets from fifteen to thirty-five pounds. Further ahead, the Riot London End of Year Party is at the same venue on Sunday the twenty-seventh of December, at the same price range.

At the B F I Southbank, The Watermelon Woman is showing in screen three on Saturday the fourteenth of November at twenty forty. Cheryl Dunye's film is a landmark of New Queer Cinema, and this screening marks thirty years since it came out. The price was not stated in the listing.

And Soho Theatre has a gay cabaret called Megasquirt: Fully Loaded. It plays the Soho Theatre Walthamstow Pub from the second to the twelfth of December, then moves to Soho Theatre on Dean Street from the fourteenth to the twenty-third of December. Prices were not given.

One-offs

Ceilidh: the musical is a premiere, directed by Sam Pinkleton, at Shoreditch Town Hall from Saturday the thirty-first of October to the thirteenth of November. It is booked through Central Tickets, and the booking fee was not stated.

Film Africa closes on Sunday the first of November at the B F I Southbank with Lady, a film about a Lagos cab driver and sex workers. It starts at six fifteen p m in screen one, with an introduction from the director and producer.

For something gentler, Kate's Knitalian is at the Air Contemporary Gallery in Soho until the fifth of December. It is a deli and trattoria rendered entirely in knit and crochet. And the British Library has opened an Agatha Christie exhibition, running until the twentieth of June, twenty twenty-seven.

Bonfire Night is covered too. Fireworks in the Park is at Wimbledon Park on Thursday the fifth of November and Morden Park on Saturday the seventh, with street food and a funfair. Battersea Park Fireworks runs on the seventh and eighth of November, and there is a Rudimental D J set on the Saturday. Prices were not stated for any of them.

Date ideas

For an evening out, the B F I Southbank has a Black Screen Classics season through October and November. Crooklyn plays from the fifth to the twenty-first of November. Babymother plays on the third and sixteenth. Touki Bouki plays on the sixth and nineteenth, and Pressure on the seventh and twenty-seventh.

Pair the pay what you can supper at The Bridge Social with a walk along the South Bank, or make an afternoon of Kate's Knitalian and a wander around Soho.

On sale soon

The big one is Loxwood Joust, which is live now for twenty twenty-seven. The dates are the weekends of the seventh and eighth, the fourteenth and fifteenth, and the twenty-first and twenty-second of August. Day passes start with an early bird price of thirty-two pounds, with five hundred available each day. After that comes the advance price, with fifteen hundred a day. A weekend camping pass is one hundred and thirty pounds, with seven hundred available for each weekend, and pre-pitched bell tents are on offer. There is also a banquet called the Feast of Champions, plus workshops. The banquet date and price could not be confirmed, and sittings are capped, so check the tickets page early.

Also on sale: The History Boys returns to London next spring. Tickets are on sale now, though the venue was not stated.

Smaller theatres

Remember, Remember! is a new musical at the Pleasance Theatre in North London, from the fourth to the fourteenth of November. It is billed as if Operation Mincemeat and Six had a child.

At the Kiln Theatre, Natasha Gordon's Nine Night returns in a new production from the twelfth of November. Sources disagree on the end date.

Chris Fung's Global Majority opens at the Bush Theatre on the twenty-fifth of November and runs to the sixteenth of January.

And Lear, with Ian McKellen and Taylor Russell, has had more performances added at The Yard. The dates were not stated.

Films

Film listings were thin this week, so just a few to know about. Sofia Coppola's Marie Antoinette is back in a four K restoration at the B F I Southbank from Friday the thirteenth of November. And Everest: The Other Side is showing at the B F I I MAX from the thirtieth of October.

Food

Starting with supper clubs. DINE is a monthly plant-based supper at The Bridge Social on Southwark Bridge Road. The next is Thursday the fifth of November at six p m, with last entry at six thirty. It is pay what you can, listed as free, at shared tables. Booking for the next one opens at six p m on the third Thursday of the month, which is the nineteenth of November.

Roobarbs Supper Club is plant-based, and has several dates. Tuesday the third of November is at Gipsy Hill Taproom in West Norwood, at thirty pounds. Thursday the fifth is at Brucan Bakery, with sittings at six thirty and eight thirty p m, at forty pounds. Friday the sixth is at Four Boroughs, also forty pounds. Festive dates follow in December. Menus were not listed, and it is worth confirming whether tickets are food only, since the taproom and Four Boroughs are drinks venues.

For a bigger night out, the E three Vegan Fine Dining Supper Club in Bethnal Green is seventy-two pounds, with dates that vary. Note that the Thanksgiving Supper Club at The Alma in Wandsworth on the twenty-sixth of November is not vegan-led.

On new places. Bubala has opened at Garrick Street in Covent Garden, since the nineteenth of October. It is mostly vegan with a separate vegan menu, and the halloumi dishes are not vegan. In Wandsworth, Take A Bite and Smile, a vegan Italian patisserie, is moving to a new permanent location, with no opening date yet. And Chippie by Jam Delish, a vegan fish and chips pop-up at Boxhall City, runs until about the twenty-eighth of November.

Comedy

Temi Wilkey's Lover Girl is at Soho Theatre Upstairs from the second to the twenty-first of November at eight thirty p m, from seventeen pounds, and it has four stars. There are Black Out Nights on Thursday the fifth and Thursday the nineteenth, with a post-show question and answer.

Nabil Abdulrashid, with Urban Battuta, is at the Top Secret Comedy Club on Drury Lane on Monday the sixteenth of November at seven p m. Limited tickets are fifteen pounds. He is a regular on Live at the Apollo and Have I Got News for You.

Also at Soho: Sophie Duker's Hot Beef Injection runs in Soho Downstairs until the seventh of November at nine fifteen p m, from twenty-one pounds. Ginger Johnson's Show Pony runs from the second to the seventh at seven fifteen p m, from twenty-four pounds. Sid Singh's Love Bombing is on Friday the sixth of November at six forty-five p m, from nineteen pounds. And Frankie Thompson's Horrible Things runs from the ninth to the twenty-first at nine p m, from thirteen pounds.

That is the week.
